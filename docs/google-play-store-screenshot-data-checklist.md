### Response analysis

Read-only review of all 22 submitted responses:

- Overall ratings were strongly positive: average satisfaction was 4.73/5, ease of navigation
  4.59/5, and likelihood to keep using the app 4.77/5. The high averages are encouraging, but
  broad star ratings do not explain where someone got stuck.
- 9 of 22 respondents selected at least one confusing feature; 13 selected “None.” Because
  this was a multi-select question, category counts overlap: Collections and Budget Tracker
  were selected 3 times each; My Animals, Enclosures, Animal Care, Marketplace, and Community
  2 times each; and Litter Management once.
- Specific comprehension/onboarding signals included uncertainty about adding siblings and
  parents efficiently, a report that a new site can be hard to learn, and a suggestion that the
  earlier Community experience was easier (the comment was unfinished). These point to asking
  what task a person was doing, what they expected to happen, and what actually happened.
- Bug reports included duplicate pairing connections after a crash, difficulty undoing
  accidental animal creation, slow or misdirected taps, trouble creating animal profiles on a
  phone, missing pedigree display, and having to log in again after returning to the app.
  These should be distinguished from “confusing” workflows in the follow-up.
- Feature suggestions included clearer or automated genetics/phenotype calculations, more
  mouse genes, multiple animal photos, keeping breeding lines separate, connecting animal
  sales with the budget, and educational content. Treat these as specific ideas to validate,
  not as evidence that every respondent needs them.
- The existing survey has 15 questions across 11 pages. Its ratings and choice questions must
  be answered to continue, while free-text answers are optional. A follow-up should be much
  shorter and ask about a concrete task rather than repeat general satisfaction ratings.

### Follow-up survey direction

Keep this to roughly 3–5 concise questions, with optional written detail:

1. Which task or area was hardest to understand? Offer the reported areas (especially
   Collections, Budget, My Animals, and Community), plus “Other” and “Nothing was difficult.”
2. What made it difficult? Options could include finding where to start, understanding a term
   or label, knowing how sections connect, entering/editing information, or understanding a
   result.
3. For the selected task, what did you expect to happen, and what happened instead? Keep this
   optional and invite answers in the respondent’s own words.
4. What would have helped most: a short guided first-use checklist, step-by-step examples,
   clearer in-context explanations, or something else?
5. Optionally ask whether the reported issue is still happening, separating bugs from
   learnability feedback.

Do not ask respondents to disclose a diagnosis. Address the expressed need with clearer
onboarding, predictable steps, and optional in-context help that benefits all new users.
